Your privacy, plainly

This site was built to delight you, not to follow you around. Here is the complete picture of what it does and does not do with your visit.

What this site never does

  • No accounts and nothing to sign up for.
  • No cookies.
  • No third-party scripts, fonts, trackers, or advertising of any kind. Every file you load comes from this domain.
  • No selling or sharing of anything about your visit with anyone.

What stays on your device

  • Your theme choice. If you pick light or dark mode, that preference is saved in your browser's local storage. It never leaves your device.
  • A small puzzle token. The contact page and the 3D experiences ask your browser to solve a tiny proof-of-work puzzle that deters spam robots. The solved token is kept in your browser's session storage and expires on its own. It is the one item on this list that does travel: it rides along with the usage pings described below, so that one visit's pings can be grouped together, and a short piece of it becomes the reference in the subject line if you email us from the contact page.
  • Your progress inside an experience, while the tab is open. Each 3D experience remembers your discovery checklist progress and whether you have already seen its little celebration messages. That lives in your browser's session storage and is cleared when the tab closes.
  • A few things that are meant to outlast the visit. Where an experience would be poorer for forgetting, it saves to your browser's local storage instead, so it is still there when you come back. The Fractal Garden saves your garden: which trees you planted, where you planted them, and how they are getting on. The garden has a Start a new garden button that clears its save on the spot. Earth Defense saves your best score along with your sound and control settings.
  • Your game of X's and O's. X's and O's saves your best score in your browser's local storage, along with the last play you called, the defense you chose to face, the colors you chose for the teams and the field, whether you turned the sound on or off, and whether you turned on Auto jump, so the game comes back the way you left it. It also keeps a game you have not finished, which is the play number, the running score, and one line for each play so far, so closing the tab halfway through does not cost you the whole game. That one is cleared as soon as the game ends, and the Start over button in the playbook clears it on the spot.
  • You can remove any of it whenever you like. Apart from that puzzle token, everything above stays on your device and is never sent to us or to anyone else. Clearing this site's data in your browser settings removes all of it at once.

What the web server keeps

Each time your browser asks this site for something, the web server writes a short line to its log, the way nearly every website does. That line includes your IP address, the time, the address of what was asked for, the page that sent you here, and the brief description your browser gives of itself, such as its name, its version, and your operating system. We use these logs only to keep the site running and secure, for example to track down a fault or turn away an attack. They are never sold or shared, never used to build a picture of who you are, and are deleted automatically within a year.

Anonymous usage counts

As visitors explore the 3D experiences, the pages send simple pings to this same domain, such as "someone found the waterfall." Each ping names the experience it came from and carries your browser's language setting, a note of whether you are on a phone or a computer, and the puzzle token that groups one visit's pings together. The pings land in a separate log of their own, which never records your IP address or the description your browser gives of itself, and are summarized into anonymized daily counts. In the published summaries each visitor is known only by a throwaway label: no names, no IP addresses, and no browser fingerprints. Some experiences display these counts inside the world itself as a little visitor dashboard.

X's and O's notes a little more, because the game is the plays. Each time you call a play, snap the ball, or throw it, and each time a play ends, it records the play and the defense, the receiver you picked, how it turned out and what it was worth, along with your running score, whether the sound was on, and whether your screen was held upright or sideways. It never records the size of your screen or anything else that could pick your device out from anyone else's.

If you email us

Your message and address are used only to reply to you. That is the whole story.
